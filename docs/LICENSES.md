# License audit

Facts from the linked sources, read on 2026-09-05. Quotes are verbatim. Anything not stated in a source is marked as such. This is not legal advice.

## LJSpeech

Source: https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/

> "This dataset is in the public domain in the US (and most likely other countries as well). There are no restrictions on its use."

> "The texts were published between 1884 and 1964, and are in the public domain. The audio was recorded in 2016-17 by the LibriVox project and is also in the public domain."

Derived data (mels, latents, alignments, subsets) can be redistributed under CC0: no restriction exists to carry over. Not used in experiment 001 (see below), kept here because AGENTS.md names it.

## LibriTTS-R (used in experiment 001)

Sources: https://www.openslr.org/141/ ("License: CC BY 4.0"), https://huggingface.co/datasets/mythicinfinity/libritts_r ("License: CC BY 4.0"). Parent LibriTTS: https://www.openslr.org/60/ ("License: CC BY 4.0").

CC BY 4.0 requires attribution and a link to the license for the material and adaptations of it. A dataset derived from it cannot be relabeled CC0. It fits the "attributed track" defined in `docs/COORDINATION.md`, not the CC0 main dataset. Per-clip provenance is written by `experiments/001-supertonic-lineage/prepare.py` to `datasets/manifests/libritts-r-dev-clean.jsonl` (git-ignored).

## Kokoro-82M (Apache 2.0 weights)

Sources: https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M (card metadata `license: apache-2.0`; the repository has no LICENSE file, checked via the HF API). Apache 2.0 text: https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0

The model card says about its own training data:

> "Kokoro was trained exclusively on permissive/non-copyrighted audio data and IPA phoneme labels. Examples of permissive/non-copyrighted audio include: Public domain audio; Audio licensed under Apache, MIT, etc; Synthetic audio[1] generated by closed[2] TTS models from large providers. [1] https://copyright.gov/ai/ai_policy_guidance.pdf [2] No synthetic audio from open TTS models or "custom voice clones""

The card contains no clause about the use of Kokoro's output. Apache 2.0 licenses the "Work" (the weights) and "Derivative Works" of it; it says nothing about output produced by running the Work. So:

- License restriction on using Kokoro output as training data: none stated.
- Model card restriction: none stated. The "[2] No synthetic audio from open TTS models" line describes what the author excluded from Kokoro's own training set, not a condition imposed on users.
- Whether TTS output is copyrightable at all, and whether a model trained on it is a "Derivative Work" of the weights under Apache 2.0: **ambiguous**. Not addressed by the license or the card.

## Supertonic 3 (OpenRAIL-M weights, MIT code)

Sources: https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3 (card: "The accompanying model is released under the OpenRAIL-M License"), license file https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3/blob/main/LICENSE (BigScience Open RAIL-M, dated August 18, 2022; identical md5 to the local copy in `~/.cache/supertonic3/LICENSE`). Code: https://github.com/supertone-inc/supertonic, MIT.

Relevant clauses, verbatim:

Definition (e):
> "“Derivatives of the Model” means all modifications to the Model, works based on the Model, or any other model which is created or initialized by transfer of patterns of the weights, parameters, activations or output of the Model, to the other model, in order to cause the other model to perform similarly to the Model, including - but not limited to - distillation methods entailing the use of intermediate data representations or methods based on the generation of synthetic data by the Model for training the other model."

Section III, paragraph 4:
> "You may host for Third Party remote access purposes (e.g. software-as-a-service), reproduce and distribute copies of the Model or Derivatives of the Model thereof in any medium, with or without modifications, provided that You meet the following conditions: a. Use-based restrictions as referenced in paragraph 5 MUST be included as an enforceable provision by You in any type of legal agreement (e.g. a license) governing the use and/or distribution of the Model or Derivatives of the Model, and You shall give notice to subsequent users You Distribute to, that the Model or Derivatives of the Model are subject to paragraph 5."

Paragraph 6:
> "The Output You Generate. Except as set forth herein, Licensor claims no rights in the Output You generate using the Model. You are accountable for the Output you generate and its subsequent uses. No use of the output can contravene any provision as stated in the License."

Attachment A lists the use restrictions (unlawful use, harming minors, disinformation, harassment, impersonation without consent, discrimination, medical advice, law enforcement uses, etc.; the full list is in the license file).

Reading:

- **Redistributing Supertonic 3 output** (audio): allowed. Paragraph 6 claims no rights in output. The output itself is not defined as a Derivative of the Model.
- **Training another model on that output**: the resulting model is a "Derivative of the Model" by definition (e), explicitly including "generation of synthetic data by the Model for training the other model". It may be distributed (paragraph 4), but only under terms that include the Attachment A use restrictions (4.a) and with a copy of the license (4.b). A model trained on Supertonic 3 output therefore **cannot be released as CC0**.
- Whether a *dataset* of Supertonic output, published on its own, must carry the restrictions: paragraph 6 says output is free of the licensor's rights, but "No use of the output can contravene any provision", and training a CC0 model on it would contravene 4.a. **Ambiguous** for the dataset alone; unambiguous for the model trained on it.
- Supertonic 3's own training data is not disclosed (see `docs/REFERENCES.md`).
